# Worked Session Cases

These are behavioral examples for frontier models. They are not scripts and should not be copied mechanically. Each case demonstrates a different organizational topology and the distinctions a good Evident 30 session should uncover.

## Case 1 — Operational team member

**Starting facts:** Chen, Logistics Coordinator, Meridian Relief Federation. Purpose: route Supply Cell work correctly.

A weak interview asks what Chen does and records the title-compatible answer.

A stronger interview follows work and neighboring distinctions:

1. What requests reach Chen?
2. What does Chen change or decide?
3. Which facts can Chen confirm directly?
4. What does Chen route elsewhere?
5. Which adjacent decisions require approval?
6. Who owns physical inventory, dispatch state, and stock reporting when these disagree?
7. What changes during an urgent exception?

Expected discovery: dispatch coordination, dispatch-state knowledge, procurement familiarity, purchase approval, stock-report ownership, and physical inventory ownership are separate relations.

Quality bar: final routing must say both where Chen is a useful route and where Chen is not.

## Case 2 — AI session operator

**Starting facts:** a customer interacts with a frontier model in a tool-enabled environment. Purpose: understand the session participant well enough to route work and distinguish capability from authority.

Do not assume a conventional employment hierarchy.

Probe:

1. What work enters through the customer?
2. Which actions are purely cognitive and which can create durable external effects?
3. Through which channels can effects occur?
4. Does technical access imply permission to use it now?
5. Who controls task intent? Who controls platform constraints?
6. What persists after the session? What does not?
7. Which responsibilities would be wrong to assign to the model merely because it can touch the relevant system?

Expected discovery: customer task authority, session-scoped operator capability, platform governance, tool-specific execution channels, external persistent systems, and explicit do-not-route boundaries.

Quality bar: preserve `knows-about != can-access != can-execute != may-execute-now != owns != accountable-for`.

## Case 3 — Founder / small-company executive

**Starting facts:** founder of a small company with product, hiring, sales, and financial involvement. Purpose: route decisions without inventing enterprise separation that does not exist.

Risk: the model may normalize one person into several hypothetical departments or assume title implies every authority.

Probe real decisions:

- Which decisions can the founder make alone?
- Which require board, investor, bank, regulator, customer, or cofounder consent?
- Which work is personally performed versus merely accountable?
- Which responsibilities are temporary because the company is small?
- Where is the founder the exception path rather than the normal route?

Expected discovery: overlapping roles can be real. The model should preserve overlap while still separating execution, approval, and accountability.

Quality bar: do not create organizational cleanliness by inventing nonexistent teams.

## Case 4 — Federated organization

**Starting facts:** a local office belongs to a global federation. Parent policies exist, but local operations and authority differ. Purpose: route a cross-boundary case.

Probe:

1. Which rule is parent-level and which is local practice?
2. What authority is inherited automatically?
3. What authority remains local?
4. What happens when parent policy conflicts with operational reality?
5. Which participant can describe the rule, and which can authorize an exception?
6. What must cross the federation boundary, and through whom?

Expected discovery: overlapping spaces, inherited rules, local authority, contested perspectives, and explicit cross-space routing.

Quality bar: disagreement between local and parent perspectives remains attributable rather than being merged into one false global fact.

## How to use these cases

Use cases to learn investigative moves, not expected answers. In a real session:

- begin from supplied facts only;
- freeze predictions before participant evidence;
- choose probes based on live uncertainty;
- inspect only authorized evidence that resolves a live distinction;
- show the emerging routing/authority map for correction;
- preserve unknowns when the organization cannot be resolved in one session.
